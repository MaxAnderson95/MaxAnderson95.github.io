# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers, engineering leaders, and technical peers — primarily platform/cloud engineers — landing on maxanderson.tech to size up Max Anderson as a senior engineer. Some arrive from a link in a resume, LinkedIn, or conference bio; others arrive from a search query for one of the technical posts (Workload Identity Federation, Kubernetes HA, RKE2, ADO pipelines). In both cases, they are evaluating competence, depth, and judgment in minutes, not skimming for entertainment.

The job to be done: in a single visit, decide whether Max is the kind of senior engineer worth a follow-up — a recruiter screen, a referral conversation, a peer-level "I want to work with this person" reaction, or simply trusting his writing on cloud-native topics.

## Product Purpose

A personal site and technical blog that establishes professional credibility through depth-of-craft. Posts demonstrate the expertise; the site itself ratifies it by feeling like something a senior engineer would build — opinionated, polished, intentional, and not pulled from a template.

Success looks like:
- A visitor who came for one blog post leaves knowing who Max is and what he does.
- A visitor who came for the bio finds technical posts that close the loop on the claims.
- The site reads as distinctly *his*, not interchangeable with the next dev-blog on the row.

## Brand Personality

Bold, futuristic, technical — read through an editorial-typographic lens. Confident but not loud, modern but not gimmicky, technical but not cosplay-hacker. The voice on the page is a senior engineer with strong takes, writing for peers who appreciate precision and don't need handholding.

Three-word personality: **bold, futuristic, technical**.

Tone of voice:
- Direct. No throat-clearing intros.
- Specific. Names the version, the flag, the exact failure mode.
- Confident without grandstanding. Doesn't oversell, doesn't undersell, doesn't apologize.
- Dry humor is welcome; performative excitement is not.

Emotional goals for the visitor: *"this person knows what they're doing"*, *"I want to read more"*, *"I want to work with this person"*.

## Anti-references

Explicitly NOT:

- **Generic SaaS gradient landing.** No purple-to-blue gradient hero. No hero-metric template (big number, supporting stats, gradient accent). No identical icon-and-heading card grids. No "Trusted by" logo wall. Anything that could be the homepage of a Series A startup.
- **Medium / Substack default.** No stock blog template, no default serif on white, no Medium-style "5 min read" chrome with no personality. The site is not a hosted blog wearing a custom domain.
- **Crypto / neon-on-black.** No neon green or pink on pure black. No glitch effects, scanlines, "matrix" rain, or hacker-aesthetic cosplay. Dark theme yes; edgelord no.
- **Corporate consulting site.** No stock photography of people pointing at laptops. No navy-and-gold trust-me palette. No "Solutions" / "Capabilities" / "Insights" IA. No vague competence-by-vibes copy.

Cross-register slop bans also apply: no side-stripe colored borders, no gradient text via `background-clip: text`, no decorative glassmorphism, no em dashes in body copy.

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't tell.** Credibility comes from the depth of the writing and the craft of the site itself, not from claims about either. If a sentence brags, cut it; let the artifact do the work.

2. **Practice what you preach.** The site should feel engineered — performant, accessible-by-default, readable on any device, clean source, no junk. A platform engineer's blog that loads slowly or breaks on mobile is its own counter-evidence.

3. **Quiet authority over loud hype.** "Bold" means committed visual choices and a distinct point of view, not big animations and exclamation marks. Senior poise: the design is confident enough not to shout.

4. **Distinct, not generic.** Every page should fail the "could be any dev blog" test. If a section could be lifted onto another personal site without anyone noticing, it isn't doing enough work.

5. **Respect the reader's time.** Content-first. No interstitials between visitor and post. Navigation, chrome, and decoration exist to serve reading, not to interrupt it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal compliance target. Best-effort, with these defaults:

- Sufficient color contrast for body copy and interactive elements on the dark theme.
- Keyboard navigability for all interactive elements; visible focus styles.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for any animations beyond subtle transitions.
- Semantic HTML and landmark structure so screen readers and reader-mode tools work without extra effort.
- Readable at standard zoom levels and on mobile viewports without horizontal scroll.
